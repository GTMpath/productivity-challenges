# 9 Ways to Be Kinder to Yourself

*From "The Productivity Project" by Chris Bailey*

---

Being kind to yourself is crucial for sustainable productivity. You can't run on empty forever. Here are nine ways to take it easy on yourself while still accomplishing more.

---

## 1. Disconnect from Productivity More Often

As a general rule, you should be taking more breaks than you are now—breaks throughout the day and breaks from work in general.

**The Science:**

Research from the University of Toronto found that when we work continuously for **52 minutes**, we should take a break for at least **17 minutes**. You should take breaks a lot more often and longer than you think.

When we feel low on energy, it's because we have a limited pool of physical energy in our brain. Once it's depleted, we become less effective at everything. Completely separating from work helps replenish that pool.

**Tip:** The best break activities let you recharge, destress, and have fun. See Challenge 7 (Disconnecting) for ideas.

---

## 2. Recall Three Things You're Grateful For

Every night, even after the toughest days, write down the three biggest things you're grateful for from that day—or if that's too hard, three things you're grateful for in general.

**Why It Works:**

According to researcher Shawn Achor, "as you consciously practice scanning for positives, your brain gets better at it and retains the new pattern of unconsciously constantly scanning the world for the good."

It's not the gratitude itself as much as the ability to scan for positives that makes this habit so effective.

For something that takes only a minute or two, the effects can be profound.

---

## 3. Journal About a Positive Experience You Had

At the end of each day, journal (or talk about) a positive experience you had. According to Shawn Achor, "your brain stamps it as meaningful."

**The Magic:**

"The brain can't tell much difference between visualization and actual experience, so you just doubled the most meaningful experiences of your day."

Over time, your brain connects the dots and you realize you have a trajectory of meaning running throughout your life.

**For lasting effects:** Give these tactics a few weeks to train your brain and become habits.

---

## 4. Break Tasks Down

There's a reason video games feel more rewarding than work: games provide a rapid succession of milestones, goals, and rewards. Most jobs are far more ambiguous and unstructured.

**The Fix:**

- Create subgoals for larger projects
- Spend more time planning to add structure
- Keep a running list of things to get done for each project

This makes your work more structured, rewarding, and engaging.

**Bonus:** Research shows this also makes you more likely to achieve a "flow" state—that magical feeling where you're so engrossed that time doesn't seem to matter.

---

## 5. Ask Yourself for Advice

One of the best ways to become more productive when facing a challenge is to ask yourself for advice.

I value the opinions of friends and family, but I always ask myself too:

> "What advice would I give myself in this situation?"

This tactic also works wonders for firing up your prefrontal cortex when you're resisting aversive tasks.

---

## 6. Reward Yourself

Whether that means:
- Spending fifteen minutes on social media after a long work session
- Transferring a dollar into a "fun money" account for every day you stick to a habit
- Treating yourself to a nice dinner when you finish a big project

Rewarding yourself works wonders for solidifying habits AND for having fun while investing in your productivity.

---

## 7. Know You Can Grow

According to Stanford psychologist Carol Dweck (author of *Mindset*), the main thing separating successful people from unsuccessful people is whether they feel their intelligence and abilities are **fixed**.

**Growth Mindset:**

People with a growth mindset believe that through hard work and persistence, they can accomplish more. They:
- Embrace obstacles as challenges to be overcome
- See working hard as the only way to master a skill
- Don't view setbacks as proof of limitation

**The Truth:**

If you feel your intelligence and abilities are set in stone, you're wrong. Remind yourself that you can always grow—it's a great way to challenge yourself to become more productive.

---

## 8. Create an Accomplishments List

Maintain an Accomplishments List that you review and add to regularly (like on Maintenance Day).

**Why It Matters:**

Before keeping this list, I invested in productivity without actually taking time to reflect on what I accomplished. Reviewing the list each week:
- Lets me pat myself on the back
- Propels me into the week ahead
- Motivates me to accomplish more—especially on long-term projects where results aren't immediately apparent

---

## 9. Look at Pictures of Cute Baby Animals

Not only can looking at cute baby animals make you go "awwwwww," it can also **boost your cognitive and motor performance**.

Yes, this is real science.

Take a break and look at some puppies or kittens. You've earned it.

---

## The Bottom Line

Productivity isn't about grinding yourself into dust. It's about accomplishing what matters and having energy left over for the rest of your life.

Be kind to yourself along the way.
