# Daily Practice

> This is a daily and weekly routine built from Chris Bailey's *The Productivity Project*, with a few additions: the weekly hours estimate and feeling score per work area, the daily energy number, and the split between active and autopilot areas. The book does not score hot spots, and neither does this vault. The 22 challenges are in the repo this vault came from, and you do not need to finish them.

## Every day: 10 to 15 minutes of overhead

**Morning, before email (5 minutes).** Open today's note and dump whatever is in your head. Then picture the end of the day and write the three things you want done by then. Bailey calls this the Rule of 3. End each one with a link to the life hotspot or work area it belongs to: type `[[` and pick the page, for example `[[Body]]`. Then decide which of the three gets your prime-time hours.

**During the day.** Do the first of your three in prime time, with 30 minutes of it offline and on that one task. Set two alarms, and when each goes off ask whether you still remember your three and whether you are on track.

**End of day (5 minutes).** Write what got done and what is carrying over. Add one win to [[Accomplishments]]. Then fill in the two properties at the top of the note: an energy number out of 10 and how many of your three got done.

## Every week: 30 minutes on maintenance day

Maintenance day is the day you batch the week's chores, and the review happens on the same day. Empty your head onto the page, then read the week's wins. Scan your hot spots list with your to-do list and calendar open, and ask which one you neglected and what should go on your radar for the next week or two. Estimate how many hours each work area got, using the links on the week's daily threes and a look at your calendar. Give each active work area a 1 to 5 for how you feel about it, and read the hours next to those scores. Answer the two autopilot questions. Finish by writing next week's three and the one thing you are saying no to.

## Active and autopilot work areas

An active area is one your results depend on. It gets weekly hours and a 1 to 5 score for how you feel about it. You need both numbers, because 20 hours with a score of 2 means the effort is not working, and 3 hours with a score of 2 means the area is being neglected.

An autopilot area is a commitment you want kept at a decent standard without much attention. It gets one fixed block a week with a cap, and two questions at the weekly review: does someone need an answer from me, and is anything broken or costing money. If both answers are no and the hours are under the cap, you can stop thinking about it until next week. This applies the book's Zenning Out challenge (#11) to a whole commitment.
