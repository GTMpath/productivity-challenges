## 1. The Values Challenge

**Time:** 7 minutes | **Energy:** 6/10 | **Value:** 8/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

Access to your deeper reasons for becoming more productive. If you're using these tactics to take more on, you could potentially save countless hours by only focusing on the productivity goals you care about. The return on this challenge can be massive.

### The Challenge

Ask yourself simple questions to identify your deepest-held values and create a plan to act in accordance with them.

**Questions to Consider:**

1. **Imagine this:** As a result of becoming more productive, you have **two more hours** of leisure time every day. How will you use that time? What new things will you take on? What will you spend more time on?

2. What productivity goals, new habits, routines, or rituals do you have in mind that you want to take on?

**Go Deeper:**

- Ask yourself: What deep-rooted values are associated with your productivity goals? Why do you want to become more productive?
- If you find yourself coming up with a lot of values you deeply care about (meaning, community, relationships, freedom, learning, etc.), chances are you care about the goal on a deep personal level
- Use the **Values → Goals → Intention → Action** framework

**Quick Shortcuts:**

- **Fill in the blank:** "I deeply care about this because ____." Spin off as many reasons as you can.
- **Deathbed test:** Fast-forward to when you're on your deathbed. Ask yourself: Would I regret doing more or less of this?

## 2. The Impact Challenge

**Time:** 10 minutes | **Energy:** 8/10 | **Value:** 10/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

You will discover the highest-impact tasks in your work, which will show you where you should invest the majority of your time, attention, and energy. **This challenge lays the groundwork for everything else.**

### The Challenge

**Of all the challenges, this is one of the most important.** It's difficult to become more productive when you don't first take stock of what you should actually be productive on.

**The Core Insight (from Brian Tracy):**
> "90 percent of the value that you contribute is contained in just three tasks."

**Steps:**

1. **Make a list of everything you're responsible for in your work.** Get everything onto paper. There's a good chance you haven't stepped back to think about everything you're responsible for on a weekly or monthly basis.

2. **Ask yourself:** If you could do just one item on that list all day, every day, what item would allow you to accomplish the most with the same amount of time? What's most valuable to your boss or yourself?

3. **Finally, ask yourself:** If you could do only two more items all day, what second and third tasks would let you accomplish the most?

These three tasks are the **20%** that contribute at least **80% of your value.**

## 3. The Rule of 3 Challenge

**Time:** 5 minutes | **Energy:** 6/10 | **Value:** 8/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

You'll step back to determine your most important tasks at the beginning of each day—the tasks where you should invest the majority of your time, attention, and energy. This gives you something to focus on so you work smarter instead of just harder.

### The Challenge

**Try the Rule of 3 tomorrow morning.**

**How to Do It:**

Before you open your inbox or start your day, sit down with pen and paper, mentally fast-forward to the end of the day, and write down the **three main things** you want to have accomplished when the day is done.

**Tips:**

- Fight the urge to check email first—you need a clear mind to think about what's important
- Think in terms of "wins, achievements, or highlights"
- Look through your calendar to see what time you'll actually have available
- Think about **when, where, and how** you'll accomplish each item
- Set two alarms during your workday to check: Are you on track?
- At the end of the day, reflect on how realistic your goals were

## 4. The Prime-Time Challenge

**Time:** ~1 week | **Energy:** 1/10 | **Value:** 9/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

An understanding of how well you manage the three ingredients of productivity—time, energy, and attention—so you know your starting point and can make adjustments.

### The Challenge

**Track your energy** to determine your BPT (Biological Prime Time), and keep a time log to evaluate how well you manage your time and attention.

**For Accurate Energy Tracking:**

Before keeping a log, you should:
- Cut out caffeine, alcohol, sugar, and other stimulants (if possible)
- Eat small, frequent meals throughout the day
- Wake up and fall asleep naturally if you can

**Every Hour, Note:**

- What time it is
- Your energy level (1-10)
- What you're doing
- How many minutes you procrastinated (estimate)

**Tools:**

- **RescueTime** (automatic tracking)
- **Toggl** (manual tracking)

**Tips:**

- If tracking for a full week feels overwhelming, try just a few days
- Don't be too hard on yourself about procrastination—be honest
- The return on this tedious exercise is huge

## 5. The Flipping Challenge

**Time:** 6 minutes | **Energy:** 8/10 | **Value:** 8/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

The ugliest tasks in your work and life will become more appealing, and you'll waste less time when working on them. This frees up more time for high-impact and meaningful tasks.

### The Challenge

The more aversive a task is, the more likely you'll put it off. Ironically, the tasks that make you most productive are often the most aversive.

**How to Flip Your Procrastination Triggers:**

The next time you notice yourself putting off a task, use that as a trigger to think about what's making it aversive. Then write those triggers down and make a plan to flip them.

**Common Procrastination Triggers (tasks that are):**

- Boring
- Frustrating
- Difficult
- Ambiguous
- Unstructured
- Lacking in personal meaning
- Lacking in intrinsic rewards

**Pro Tip:** Create a "procrastination list" of the costs of putting off the task. Or just get started—action creates momentum.

## 6. The Time-Traveling Challenge

**Time:** 10 minutes | **Energy:** 4/10 | **Value:** 7/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

You'll become less likely to put things off until tomorrow because you'll stop seeing your future self as a stranger.

### The Challenge

When you put something off or waste time, you're almost always being unfair to your future self.

**Future-Self Continuity:**

Ask yourself: How connected do you feel to your future self? (Some people see their future self as almost a stranger; others feel deeply connected.)

**Ways to Connect with Your Future Self:**

- **Download AgingBooth** or a similar app to see what you'll look like older
- **Send a letter to future you** through FutureMe.org
- **Create a future memory:** Imagine your future self and visualize a specific moment

Trust me: You'll thank yourself later.

## 7. The Disconnecting Challenge

**Time:** 30 minutes | **Energy:** 1/10 | **Value:** 10/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

You'll waste much less time and find yourself working on higher-impact and meaningful tasks more often.

### The Challenge

**Disconnect from the internet for thirty minutes tomorrow.**

Whether that means putting your phone on airplane mode during dinner, disconnecting from Wi-Fi while working on an important task, or going offline when your energy is low (so you're not tempted to waste time)—disconnect, and observe how much you get done.

**Why This Works:**

When you notice yourself surfing the internet on autopilot, use that as a trigger to disconnect. This prevents wasted time and lets you ease back into focused work.

**A Word to the Wise:**

When you disconnect, you may feel bored as your brain adjusts to lower stimulation. That's your limbic system begging for the dopamine hit that being online provides.

**Don't listen to it.**

## 8. The Shrink Your Work Challenge

**Time:** 1 minute | **Energy:** 4/10 | **Value:** 8/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

You'll learn to expend more energy and attention on your work to get things done in a fraction of the time.

### The Challenge

**Tomorrow, limit how much time you'll spend on an important task—and stick to that limit.**

**How to Do It:**

Set a countdown timer for about **half** the time you think a task will take. If you think you need four hours for a presentation, schedule just two—during your Biological Prime Time if possible.

**When This Works Best:**

This tactic works wonders on tasks that are important and have a deadline approaching. It forces you to focus.

**Key Insight:**

When you consistently work long hours or spend too much time on tasks, that's usually not a sign you have too much to do—it's a sign you're not spending your energy and attention wisely.

## 9. The Working in Prime Time Challenge

**Time:** 5 minutes | **Energy:** 4/10 | **Value:** 8/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

You'll get your highest-impact tasks done more efficiently because you'll work on them when you actually have the most energy—instead of when they feel urgent.

### The Challenge

**Tomorrow, let your Biological Prime Time and energy levels dictate what you work on.**

**How to Do It:**

- During your BPT, schedule time to work on your three most important tasks
- Be defensive of your BPT—that time is yours to be productive
- Block off your BPT in your calendar so no one books you during that time
- Adapt on the fly if your energy is higher or lower than usual

**Key Insight:**

Your productivity per hour is not consistent—it depends on how much energy and attention you have. Not all hours of the day are created equal.

**Your prime time is sacred; use it wisely.**

## 10. The Maintenance Challenge

**Time:** 10 minutes+ | **Energy:** 2/10 | **Value:** 7/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

You'll carve out more attention and energy for important work throughout the week. You'll also procrastinate less, experience more mental clarity, and feel great when you tackle a dozen maintenance tasks at once.

### The Challenge

**Establish a Maintenance Day ritual.**

**Week 1: Collect**

Continue doing maintenance tasks throughout the week, but write them down if you think you can safely batch them later. (Some things like watering plants may not wait.)

**Week 2: Batch**

Schedule a Maintenance Day (or half-day) to tackle all those tasks at once. During that time, use spare mental energy for something meaningful like listening to a podcast or learning a language.

**Important:** Don't tackle maintenance tasks during your BPT—that time is sacred!

### The Challenge

**Choose one or two low-impact support tasks that you have trouble taming, and shrink them** by setting a limit for how much time you spend on them, how frequently you focus on them, or both.

**Resist the urge!** That misplaced guilt goes away with time.

**Reward Yourself:**

## 13. The Capture Challenge

**Time:** 20-30 minutes | **Energy:** 9/10 | **Value:** 9/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

This is perhaps the most fun and freeing challenge. You'll clear up incredible attentional space, become able to fully invest in your work, and feel calmer knowing nothing will slip through the cracks.

### The Challenge

**Perform the brain dump ritual.**

**How to Do It:**

1. **Shut off all your devices** and sit down with only a notepad and pen
2. **Capture everything in your mind** that bubbles up—tasks, projects, worries, things that slipped through the cracks, things you're waiting for
3. Once you've captured everything, start managing it in an external system

**Why This Works:**

Smaller changes are more likely to stick. The value of capturing everything is immense, but don't dive in too deep. Just get it out of your head and onto paper.

## 16. The Notification Challenge

**Time:** 5-30 minutes | **Energy:** 3/10 | **Value:** 3/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

You'll potentially reclaim hours of lost productivity every day because you'll no longer be interrupted by alerts. You'll also remember more and focus more deeply.

### The Challenge

Every interruption costs you about half an hour in lost productivity. It's worth taming them.

**Dive into the settings on your phone, computer, tablet, smartwatch, and every other device, and disable notification alerts on them all.** Shut off buzzes, beeps, alert boxes, and anything that hijacks your attention.

**Be Especially Defensive During BPT:**

If you like to remain accessible, your BPT is the best time to close yourself off.

**Expect Resistance:**

Your limbic system will feel the loss of constant stimulation. You might even feel less productive because your brain isn't as stimulated. But when the dust settles, you'll have much more attention for meaningful work.

## 17. The Single-Tasking Challenge

**Time:** 15-30 minutes | **Energy:** 9/10 | **Value:** 10/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

You'll build up your attention muscle so you can devote more focus to the task at hand and get more done in the same amount of time.

### The Challenge

**Spend between fifteen and thirty minutes tomorrow focusing on just one thing.**

Whether you dedicate all your attention to work, a phone call, a conversation, a book, or a meal—for 15-30 minutes, focus on just that one thing.

**How to Do It:**

Whenever your mind wanders—and it will—gently bring it back. Don't be too hard on yourself; simply redirect your focus.

**Why This Works:**

This is a simple challenge, but deceivingly so. Over time, the benefits compound and will blow you away. Single-tasking is one of the best ways to tame a wandering mind.

## 18. The Meditation Challenge

**Time:** 5 min/day for 7 days | **Energy:** 9/10 | **Value:** 9/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

You'll build up your attention muscle even more than with single-tasking, which lets you keep long-term goals in mind and act more deliberately.

### The Challenge

**Work out your attention muscle for five minutes every day for the next seven days,** either by meditating or integrating mindfulness into your day.

**Why Your Brain Resists This:**

Since meditation trains your mind to overpower your brain's instinctual limbic system, that limbic system will put up a fight. But every time you bring your wandering mind back to focus on your breath, your attention muscle gets stronger.

**Making It Fun:**

Give this experiment a shot for just a few days and observe what happens—especially how different you think and feel afterward. You can do anything for five minutes.

**Plant a Microintention:**

Right now, think of a trigger you'll use tomorrow to step back from your work and reflect on how you're feeling, thinking, and what you intend to accomplish.

**The Payoff:**

It's not easy to train your attention muscle, but that's what makes it worthwhile. It's incredible when you train your brain to conquer itself.

## 19. The Lamest Diet Challenge

**Time:** 2 minutes | **Energy:** 2/10 | **Value:** 8/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

Your energy levels will be more constant because you'll provide your body and brain with steady glucose throughout the day.

### The Challenge

**Make one small incremental improvement to how you eat**—a change that will cause you to either eat more unprocessed foods or become more mindful of when you're full.

**The Philosophy:**

The best diet in the world is the exact one you already have, with one small improvement. You won't drop inches overnight, but you'll actually stick with it. That's what matters.

**Ideas:**

- No sugar in your morning coffee
- Snack on vegetables instead of chips
- Don't eat in front of your computer so you notice when you're full
- Eat dinner with family instead of in front of the TV

**Why Small Changes Work:**

Small changes don't suck up all your willpower, which gives you energy to make further improvements when the time comes. Week after week, month after month, they add up.

## 20. The Water Challenge

**Time:** 2 minutes | **Energy:** 2/10 | **Value:** 7/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

Fewer energy crashes throughout the day and more overall energy throughout the week. Energy is the fuel you burn to be productive.

### The Challenge

**This week, make one small incremental improvement to what you drink.**

**Ideas:**

- **Consume fewer sugary drinks.** They spike glucose and cause energy crashes.
- **Reduce your caffeine tolerance.** This helps you drink caffeine strategically later.
- **Drink caffeine strategically**—before hunkering down on a pressing task, or between 9:30-11:30am when most people need it most.
- **Drink less alcohol.** You'll be surprised how much more energy you have.

**The Connection:**

What's good for your brain is good for your body. When what you drink has such a profound effect on energy, it's worth a second look.

## 22. The Sleeping Challenge

**Time:** 5 minutes | **Energy:** 7/10 | **Value:** 9/10

### What You'll Get Out of It

You'll save time because you'll be able to expend more energy (instead of time) on your work, making you more efficient. You'll also experience more mental clarity, concentration, short-term memory, and problem-solving skills.

### The Challenge

**Reflect on whether you're getting enough sleep each night, and if not, make a plan to fix that.**

**Good Starting Point:**

Ask yourself whether you feel the need to catch up on sleep over the weekend. If you do, you probably aren't getting enough during the week.

**Creating a Bedtime Ritual:**

1. Pick a very specific target time to be in bed
2. Work backward from that time to plan your ritual

**Things to Watch Out For:**

- **Blue light exposure:** Limit screens before bed
- **Caffeine:** Be mindful up to ten hours before sleeping
- **Sleep environment:** Keep it cool and comfortable

**The Productivity Cost:**

Although cutting back on sleep saves time, any time you save below what your body requires isn't worth it.

> **For every hour of sleep you miss, you lose at least two hours of productivity.**
